What is Challenger call analysis?

Challenger call analysis measures whether sales conversations show the three Challenger behaviors — teaching a new perspective, tailoring it to the buyer’s situation, and taking control of the conversation and next steps — and where those behaviors break down.

What Bylda tracks

On every call, automatically.

Observable conduct only — each finding links to the moment in the recording, so anyone can check it.

  • Whether an insight was shared before the pitch
  • Whether the insight referenced the buyer’s own situation
  • What the rep did after the buyer pushed back — held, reframed or retreated
  • Who set the next step, and whether it had a date
  • Changes in pace and interruptions under pressure

Where it breaks down

Three patterns worth catching early.

Teaching turns into pitching

The insight is delivered as product features instead of a new view of the buyer’s problem.

Retreat after pushback

The buyer challenges the reframe and the rep immediately agrees and moves on.

Control handed back at the end

The buyer sets the next step, often “send me something.”

FAQ

Challenger call analysis, answered.

What are the three Challenger behaviors?

Teach — share an insight that changes how the buyer sees their problem; tailor — connect it to their situation and stakeholders; take control — lead the conversation, including pricing and next steps.

Where does Challenger most often break down?

Frequently right after the buyer pushes back. Reps trained to challenge often retreat under live pressure, which shows up as a quick concession, faster speech and a next step set by the buyer.

Can you measure “taking control” objectively?

Partly. Observable signs include who proposes the next step, whether it has a date and owner, whether the rep asks for commitment, and whether pricing is discussed on the rep’s terms.

Does Bylda work with other methodologies too?

Yes. Bylda measures observable conduct, so the same analysis applies to MEDDPICC, SPIN, NEPQ or your own process.
