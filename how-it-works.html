How It Works

Trace. Signal. Echo.
Directive. Outcome.

Five stages, each grounded in the one before it. The vocabulary is precise because the steps are genuinely different from one another — and because the last one is what makes the first four honest.

Before anything: connect

Bylda reads from the systems your conversations already live in — your conferencing platform, dialer, calendar, inbox, and CRM. OAuth connection, scoped to what it needs, reviewed before the first read. There is no bot to invite to meetings, nothing for a rep to switch on, and no migration.

Stage 01

Trace — the evidence.

A Trace is one piece of behavioral evidence detected inside a conversation. Raw, timestamped, and not yet interpreted.

Traces are deliberately atomic. They are facts about a recording — the rep interrupted here, the buyer paused there, price was stated at this second, this question was asked and that one wasn't. On their own they mean very little, which is the point: everything above this layer has to be derived from something checkable.

Stage 02

Signal — what mattered.

A Trace becomes a Signal when context makes it consequential. Same evidence, now interpreted against the shape of the conversation and the state of the deal.

This is where most of the judgment lives. A two-second pause before answering an objection is a Trace; it becomes a Signal only in a conversation where the objection was substantive and the answer that followed was long and unprompted. The same Trace in a different conversation means nothing, and Bylda should say nothing.

Signals come in kinds — risk, buying, coaching, behavioral — and every one carries the Traces it was built from, so a rep can disagree with the interpretation and check the evidence.

Stage 03

Echo — what repeats.

A Signal in one conversation is an anecdote. The same Signal across many conversations is an Echo — and Echoes are the only thing worth changing a process over.

Price before criteria → ECHO 9 of this rep's 18 conversations
Objection answered under 2 seconds → ECHO 4 reps, concentrated in stalled deals
Signer absent from every call → ECHO Most stalled opportunities this quarter

This is the step that turns call analysis into organizational intelligence. One rep's habit is a coaching conversation. The same habit on four reps is an enablement gap, a hiring profile, or a broken step in your sales process — and you would never find it one call at a time.

Echoes are always grounded in counted, repeated evidence. Nothing here is a vibe.

Stage 04

Directive — the change.

One specific, executable change. Expressed as an action rather than a quality, ranked so there is only ever one, and carrying the evidence that produced it.

  • Derived from an Echo, never from a single call
  • Phrased as something to do on the next call, not a trait to improve
  • One per rep at a time — behavior change has a throughput limit
  • Evidence attached, so the rep can disagree and check

Stage 05 — Outcome, and why it is the important one

Every stage above is a claim. The Outcome is the only thing that tests them.

Bylda records what happened after the directive: meeting booked or not, deal advanced, stalled, won, lost, renewed, churned. Then it asks the question almost no coaching tool asks about itself — did the reps who changed this behavior actually see different results?

A directive that stops working stops being given. Software that cannot do that is just an opinion with a login.

Over enough cycles the loop produces something specific to you: an account of which behaviors precede revenue in your market, with your buyers, at your price point. That accumulation is the Behavior Graph, and it is the part of Bylda that cannot be bought or copied.

One honest caveat, stated in the product as well as here: observed association is not proof of cause. What the loop supports is a well-evidenced claim worth acting on and re-measuring — not a guarantee. Where the evidence is still thin, Bylda says so rather than inventing confidence.
