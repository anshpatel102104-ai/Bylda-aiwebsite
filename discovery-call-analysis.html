What is discovery call analysis?

Discovery call analysis reviews first and early sales conversations for the depth and order of questioning — situation, problem, consequence, decision criteria and process — and whether the rep followed up on answers before moving to a pitch or pricing.

What Bylda tracks

On every call, automatically.

Observable conduct only — each finding links to the moment in the recording, so anyone can check it.

  • Number and type of questions, and how many were followed up
  • Whether the buyer described the cost of doing nothing
  • Whether decision criteria and process were confirmed
  • When pricing came up relative to discovery
  • Whether the call ended with a scheduled next step

Where it breaks down

Three patterns worth catching early.

The questionnaire call

Many questions, no follow-up — the rep collects answers without going deeper.

Pricing too early

The buyer asks for a number and the rep gives one before criteria are known.

No consequence

The problem is named but never sized, so there’s nothing to justify change.

FAQ

Discovery call analysis, answered.

What makes a good discovery call?

Open questions that get followed up, a problem the buyer describes in their own words, an understanding of what it costs them to do nothing, a confirmed decision process — and a scheduled next step.

How many questions should a rep ask on a discovery call?

There’s no magic number. Follow-up matters more than count: eleven questions with no follow-up often uncover less than six that each go a level deeper.

When should pricing come up in discovery?

Usually after the problem, its cost and the decision criteria are understood. Early pricing isn’t always wrong, but it’s worth checking how often it appears before deals stall in your own data.

How do you measure discovery quality at scale?

Analyze every discovery call for the same observable markers and compare calls that progressed with those that didn’t, rather than reviewing a handful by hand.
