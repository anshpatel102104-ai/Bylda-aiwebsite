The Behavior Graph

Your sales intelligence learns how your team wins.

Analyzing a call is table stakes. The hard part is connecting a behavior to the action a rep took and the outcome that followed — thousands of times, inside one company — until you know which behaviors actually produce revenue here.

Why "we use AI" is not a moat

We should say this plainly, because it is the question a serious buyer asks third: the models underneath every product in this category are broadly available, improving fast, and converging. Nobody's advantage is the model. Anyone claiming otherwise is describing a temporary gap.

What does not commoditize is a specific, accumulated record of behavior → action → outcome inside one organization. That data does not exist anywhere else, cannot be bought, and gets more valuable the longer it runs.

The moat is not the analysis. It is knowing which analysis turned out to be right, here, with these buyers.

What the Behavior Graph connects

Four things, joined:

  • Seller behavior — what the rep did, in what order, at what pace.
  • Buyer behavior — what the other side did in response.
  • The action taken — the directive given, and whether it was followed.
  • The outcome — booked, advanced, stalled, won, lost, renewed, churned.

Joined across enough conversations, that structure supports a question no scorecard can answer: when this objection appears, and the seller responds this way, at this point in the process, with this type of buyer — what usually happens next?

The join

Behavior, and what followed.

Every row is one observed behavior paired with the outcome that actually resolved. Rows accumulate. Patterns emerge from your data, not from a template.

Rows shown are illustrative. Bylda does not publish ratios or lift figures it has not measured in your organization, and it will tell you when a pattern is still too thin to act on.

The flywheel, stated honestly

More conversations produce more traces. More traces produce better signal detection. Better signals produce sharper directives. Directives produce outcomes. Outcomes tell the graph which directives were right — which improves the next set of signals.

That loop is real and it compounds. It is also slow at the start, and we would rather say so than imply otherwise. In week one Bylda is a very good auditor. The behavioral advantage specific to your company accrues over quarters, not days.

What it becomes

  • Rep-specific — the behaviors that work for this rep, given how they actually sell.
  • Team-specific — the patterns separating your strongest reps from the rest.
  • Company-specific — the sequence that works on your buyers, at your price point.
  • Vertical-specific — eventually, how the answer differs by market.

What we are not claiming

The Behavior Graph is the architecture Bylda is built on and the intelligence it accumulates for you. It is not a pre-trained oracle of universal sales truth, it does not hold millions of other companies' interactions, and it does not predict individual deal outcomes with certainty. Where the evidence is thin, the product says so. See where Bylda is going for the honest split between what ships today and what does not.
