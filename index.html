Buyers

Stop treating every buyer
like the same deal.

Three companies, considering the same offer. The fields look identical. The evidence doesn't — so the right next move is different for each.

Buyer A · Same offer

The rollout is the risk

  • Asked twice how long implementation takes
  • Mentioned a tool that failed to launch last year
  • Wants a reference from a similar company

Bylda suggests

Lead with the rollout plan and a reference call. Don't open with price — it isn't the question being asked.

Buyer B · Same offer

The board is the audience

  • Asked for cost-per-seat and payback period
  • Said leadership approves anything over $50k
  • Forwards your emails internally

Bylda suggests

Send a one-page business case written for the leadership meeting — the buyer needs something to forward, not another demo.

Buyer C · Same offer

Interested, but not now

  • Opens everything, replies thoughtfully
  • “Maybe next quarter” — twice
  • No budget event on the horizon

Bylda suggests

Stop pushing for meetings. Share one relevant proof point a month, and watch for the signal that timing changed.

These aren't personality types. It's the evidence in each relationship — and it changes as new signals arrive.

Reps aren't interchangeable users, and the best ones don't sell from a script. Bylda learns each rep's workflow and follow-up habits from their actual calls and pipeline — then helps them prepare, catches commitments before they slip, and points out the specific pattern worth fixing.

Coaching grounded in observed behavior, not a personality test — and not a leaderboard.
