How It Works

Every conversation leaves a trace.

Bylda turns raw behavioral evidence into something a person can act on this afternoon. Five steps, each one grounded in the one before it.

01 Trace A single piece of behavioral evidence detected inside a conversation. Rep interrupted the buyer 0.4s after the word “budget.”
02 Signal A trace that matters, once Bylda has the context around it. The buyer's questions stopped after price was introduced.
03 Echo A signal that repeats — across calls, reps, accounts, or outcomes. This pattern appears in most of the team's stalled deals.
04 Directive The specific change Bylda recommends, with the evidence attached. Establish decision criteria before discussing price.
05 Outcome What happened next — and the only honest test of the directive. Meeting booked. Deal advanced. Deal lost.

Outcomes feed back into the first step. A directive that stops working stops being given — which is the part no static scorecard can do.

The Phantom Audit

Every conversation becomes evidence.

One call goes in. What comes out is not a transcript, a score, or a summary — it is a finding, and the behavior behind it.

One sales conversation Traces — what was said, when, by whom, in what order Signals — which of those mattered to this deal Echoes — what it repeats across the team A directive, and the evidence for it

Bylda reads what it can observe: question quality and count, discovery depth and duration, interruptions, listening ratio, pacing, the order in which pricing and criteria came up, who was actually on the call, and the language of commitment or resistance. It does not claim to read minds, and it does not score emotion.

The Behavior Graph

Your sales intelligence learns how your team wins.

Any model can analyze a call. The hard part — and the part that compounds — is connecting a behavior to the action a rep took and the outcome that followed, thousands of times, inside one company.

Discovery ran long before pricing came up → OUTCOME Second meeting booked
Pricing objection answered immediately, at length → OUTCOME Deal stalled
Implementation questions asked, signer present → OUTCOME Deal advanced
Criteria confirmed before the demo → OUTCOME Deal won

Enough of these and the question changes. Not “what happened on that call?” but “which behaviors actually produce revenue here, with these buyers, in this market?” That answer is specific to your company, it cannot be bought, and it gets sharper every week you use it.
