About

Sales software measured activity.
It never measured behavior.

Bylda exists because the variable that decides revenue — how people actually conduct themselves in a conversation — has never been something software could see.

The observation

Sit with a sales manager on a Friday and watch them try to work out why one rep is at 140% and another at 60%. They have the activity data. Calls made, meetings booked, pipeline created, win rate, deal velocity. All of it accurate, none of it explanatory.

So they do the only thing available: they listen to a few calls. Five, maybe, out of the three hundred the team had that week. Whatever is systematically going wrong is, by arithmetic, mostly happening in the 295 they never hear.

Then they coach from that sample, and everyone involved knows it is a sample.

The information was always there. It was in the conversations. Nobody had the hours to go and find it.

What changed

Two things, recently and quietly. Almost every sales conversation is now recorded by default. And models can now read a conversation the way an experienced manager does — noticing the question that was never asked, the objection answered too quickly, the order things happened in — rather than just transcribing the words.

Those two facts together make behavior measurable for the first time. Not inferred from a survey, not self-reported in a CRM field, but observed directly, on every conversation, every day.

What we decided to build

Not a better recorder — recording is commoditized and was never the valuable part. Not another dashboard — the world does not need more numbers without reasons.

We built the layer that reads what is underneath the record. We call it the Phantom Layer: the behavioral stratum beneath the visible pipeline, always present, never previously captured. Bylda audits it continuously and returns findings a person can act on today.

The category name for this is behavioral sales intelligence, and the longer arc — including a plain account of what does not ship yet — is on our vision page.

Principles

Five positions we will not trade.

  • Every claim is traceable. If Bylda says a behavior happened, it points at the moment. A finding you cannot verify is an assertion, and assertions do not change how anyone sells.
  • We measure conduct, not feelings. Observable behavior only. No emotion scoring, no personality typing, no claims about what a buyer was feeling. That is contested science, it produces numbers that look precise and are not, and in workplace settings it is increasingly restricted. Conduct is measurable and it is the part a rep can change.
  • Reps come first. A rep sees their own audit before their manager does. No leaderboards. A tool a sales team resents produces gamed behavior and worthless data, which defeats the entire point.
  • Your data is yours. Conversations never train shared or third-party models. That is contractual, not a policy page — the Behavior Graph built from your data belongs to you.
  • We say what it does not do. On this site, on demos, and when the honest answer is that we are not the right fit yet.

Why "Phantom"

Not because anything is spooky. Because the most important information in a sales process has been invisible — present in every conversation, recorded by nobody, acted on by almost no one. Phantom is what we call the intelligence that was always there and finally became legible.

We use the word sparingly and only where it means something: the Phantom Layer and the call audit that reads it. Everything else in the product has a normal name, because renaming ordinary things is how companies become tiresome.

Where we are

Early, and honest about it. Bylda is opening access in waves to B2B sales organizations where calls are recorded, a CRM is in use, and a manager has more reps than listening hours. If that is you, the fastest way to evaluate us is to bring a week of real conversations and see whether the findings are ones you would have reached yourself with unlimited time.

Bring a week of real calls.

That is genuinely the whole evaluation. Either the findings are useful or they are not.
