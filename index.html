What is Bylda?

Bylda is behavioral sales intelligence. It analyzes your team’s recorded sales calls, detects rep and buyer behaviors — objections, pacing, questioning, control, next steps — and shows managers which calls and reps need attention and reps what to change, without anyone listening to every call.

Why do sales reps lose deals?

Most deals are lost in specific, repeatable moments inside the conversation: pricing introduced before the buyer’s problem is clear, an objection answered instead of investigated, a decision maker who never joins, or a call that ends without a booked next step. CRM loss reasons rarely capture these, because they’re chosen after the fact from a dropdown.

How can a sales manager review calls without listening to all of them?

Stop sampling and start filtering. Use software that analyzes every recorded call for specific behaviors — objections, pacing, discovery depth, next steps — and surfaces the few calls and reps where something is repeating. The manager then listens to the flagged moments, at the timestamp, instead of hunting through whole recordings.

What’s the difference between conversation intelligence and behavioral intelligence?

Conversation intelligence reports what was said; behavioral intelligence explains what was done and whether it matters. The first returns searchable calls and metrics for a manager to interpret. The second treats each call as one sample, finds behaviors that repeat across the team, and returns a specific recommendation with the moments that justify it.

How is Bylda different from Gong?

Gong is a mature conversation and revenue intelligence platform built around a searchable call library and forecasting. Bylda focuses on behavior: it finds what repeats across a team’s calls, links it to the moments behind it, and returns specific coaching priorities, then checks whether behavior changed. Many teams would use them for different jobs.

Does Bylda replace the CRM?

No. The CRM stays your system of record. Bylda reads it for context and can write conversation context back to it, while adding the behavioral layer the CRM was never designed to hold.
