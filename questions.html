Sales behavior questions, answered plainly.

36 questions sales leaders ask about why deals stall, how to coach without listening to every call, and how behavioral intelligence differs from conversation intelligence. Short answers first; detail one click away.

Updated September 2026

The short version

  1. Deals are usually lost in specific, repeatable moments inside the conversation, not in the CRM fields that record them.
  2. Managers can’t listen to every call, so the job is filtering: find the calls and reps where a behavior repeats.
  3. Measure observable conduct — questions, objections, pacing, next steps — and compare it with your own won and lost deals.

Why deals are lost or stall

Why do sales reps lose deals?

Most deals are lost in specific, repeatable moments inside the conversation: pricing introduced before the buyer’s problem is clear, an objection answered instead of investigated, a decision maker who never joins, or a call that ends without a booked next step. CRM loss reasons rarely capture these, because they’re chosen after the fact from a dropdown.

How do you find out why sales deals stall?

Look at the last two or three conversations before the stall, not the stage history. Check whether the next step was booked with a date, whether the economic buyer ever attended, whether an objection was raised and left unresolved, and whether the buyer’s questions stopped. Stalls usually show up in behavior weeks before they show up in the pipeline.

What is deal slippage, and how do you prevent it?

Deal slippage is when an opportunity’s close date keeps moving to later periods. It’s prevented earlier than most teams try: by confirming the decision process and who signs before pricing, booking every next step with a date and owner, and catching the calls where control shifted to the buyer — “send me something” with nothing scheduled.

Why do prospects go quiet after a demo?

Prospects usually go quiet when the demo answered questions they hadn’t asked yet. Common patterns: discovery was cut short, the demo covered features rather than the problem the buyer described, and the call ended with a follow-up email instead of a scheduled decision step. The fix is in the call before the demo, not the chase afterward.

How do you tell from a sales call that a deal is at risk?

Listen for behavior, not sentiment: the buyer stops asking questions, a new approver appears late, pricing is challenged and the rep rushes past it, stakeholders who were on earlier calls stop attending, or the rep accepts “send me something” without setting a date. One of these is a moment; several repeating across calls is a risk pattern.

What does “send me something” mean on a sales call?

“Send me something” usually means the buyer is ending the conversation politely without committing to a next step. It’s often associated with deals that stall. A stronger response is to agree, then ask what they’d need to see to decide and book a time to review it together, so control of the next step stays shared.

Coaching without listening to every call

How can a sales manager review calls without listening to all of them?

Stop sampling and start filtering. Use software that analyzes every recorded call for specific behaviors — objections, pacing, discovery depth, next steps — and surfaces the few calls and reps where something is repeating. The manager then listens to the flagged moments, at the timestamp, instead of hunting through whole recordings.

Read more →

How many sales calls should a manager review each week?

Most managers can realistically review three to five full calls a week, which is a small fraction of what a team of eight reps produces. The number matters less than which calls: coaching from a random sample misses the patterns that repeat in the calls nobody hears. Prioritize calls where a known behavior repeated.

What should a sales manager coach first?

Coach the behavior that repeats most across a rep’s recent calls and appears most often before lost or stalled deals — one thing at a time. A rep given five things to fix usually fixes none. Tie the coaching to two or three specific call moments so the rep can hear exactly what changed.

How do you coach reps on objection handling?

Start from real calls, not scripts. Find the moments where the rep answered an objection immediately, then compare them with calls where the rep paused, acknowledged the concern and asked one diagnostic question. Practice that specific sequence, then check the rep’s next real calls to see whether it held under pressure.

Does call scoring improve sales performance?

A score on its own rarely changes behavior, because it doesn’t tell the rep what to do differently. Scores are useful for trends; coaching needs the specific moment and the better move. Reps improve faster from “you speed up when price comes up — pause and ask one question” than from “67 out of 100.”

How do you give sales reps feedback that changes behavior?

Make it specific, timestamped and singular: one behavior, the call moments that show it, and a concrete alternative to try. Let the rep see their own findings first, then check their next real calls to confirm the change. Feedback tied to evidence the rep can replay is far harder to dismiss than opinion.

Measuring sales behavior

What sales behaviors are associated with winning deals?

It differs by team and market, which is why generic benchmarks mislead. Behaviors worth measuring in your own won and lost deals include discovery depth before pricing, whether decision criteria were confirmed, how objections were handled, whether the signer attended, and whether every call ended with a scheduled next step. Compare your own outcomes, not someone else’s averages.

Read more →

How do you measure discovery call quality?

Measure what happened, not how long it lasted: how many open questions were asked, whether the rep followed up on answers, whether the buyer described a consequence of doing nothing, whether decision criteria and process were covered, and whether pricing waited until after. Compare these across calls that did and didn’t progress.

Read more →

How do you track objection handling across a sales team?

Tag each objection by type — price, timing, authority, competitor, status quo — then record what the rep did next: answered immediately, acknowledged, or asked a diagnostic question, and how long they spoke afterward. Tracked across the team, this shows which objections are rising and which responses appear before deals that progress.

What is a good talk-to-listen ratio in sales?

There isn’t one universal number, and commonly cited benchmarks vary by call type — discovery calls need more listening than demos. A more useful question is when the rep talks: long monologues right after an objection or a pricing question are a stronger warning sign than the overall ratio. Compare ratios against your own winning calls.

Does a rep’s speaking pace matter on sales calls?

Pace matters most when it changes. Many reps speed up under pressure — after a price objection, for example — and that shift is more telling than their average pace. A sudden increase often comes with interruptions and over-explaining, and it’s one of the easiest behaviors for a rep to fix once they can see it.

How do you measure MEDDPICC adherence on calls?

Check each call for evidence of each element — metrics, economic buyer, decision criteria, decision process, paper process, identified pain, champion and competition — rather than relying on CRM fields reps fill in afterward. The useful view is which elements are skipped most often, by which reps, and in which deals that later stalled.

Conversation intelligence vs. behavioral intelligence

What is conversation intelligence?

Conversation intelligence is software that records, transcribes and indexes sales calls, then reports on what was said — topics, keywords, talk ratios and trackers. It makes calls searchable and reviewable. The interpretation — what the pattern means and what a rep should change — is usually left to the manager.

Read more →

What is behavioral sales intelligence?

Behavioral sales intelligence analyzes how reps and buyers behave in sales conversations — objections, questioning, pacing, control, next steps — finds which behaviors repeat across a team, and connects them to the outcomes that followed. It tells managers what to coach and reps what to change, without anyone listening to every call.

Read more →

What’s the difference between conversation intelligence and behavioral intelligence?

Conversation intelligence reports what was said; behavioral intelligence explains what was done and whether it matters. The first returns searchable calls and metrics for a manager to interpret. The second treats each call as one sample, finds behaviors that repeat across the team, and returns a specific recommendation with the moments that justify it.

What is revenue intelligence?

Revenue intelligence aggregates CRM, activity and pipeline data to forecast revenue and flag at-risk deals. It answers which deals are at risk and what the number will be. It generally doesn’t explain the behavior inside the calls that caused the risk, which is where behavioral intelligence focuses.

Do you need Gong to improve sales coaching?

Not necessarily. A call recorder with search is useful if managers have time to review calls. If the bottleneck is that nobody can listen to enough calls, the need is analysis that tells managers which calls and behaviors matter. Some teams run both: a recorder as the library, behavioral intelligence as the coach.

What are alternatives to Gong for sales coaching?

Alternatives fall into three groups: other conversation intelligence platforms that record and index calls, revenue intelligence platforms focused on forecasting, and behavioral intelligence tools like Bylda that analyze recorded calls to recommend what each rep should change. Choose by the job: a searchable library, a forecast, or coaching priorities.

AI, data and trust

Can AI analyze sales calls accurately?

AI is reliable at observable conduct — who spoke when, questions asked, interruptions, pace, whether pricing or next steps came up — and less reliable when it infers feelings or intent. The trustworthy approach is to report conduct, link every finding to the exact timestamp, and let people verify it by listening.

Read more →

Does AI sales coaching actually work?

It works when it produces one specific, evidence-backed change per rep and checks whether that change showed up in later calls. It doesn’t work when it generates generic advice — “ask more open questions” — from a single transcript. The test is whether rep behavior on real calls changes, not whether the advice sounds right.

Can AI detect emotion or sentiment on sales calls?

Some tools claim to, but inferring emotion from voice is contested science and increasingly restricted in workplace settings. Bylda deliberately doesn’t score emotion. It reports observable conduct — what was said, when, and in what order — which is measurable, verifiable at a timestamp, and something a rep can actually change.

Is it legal to record and analyze sales calls?

Generally yes, when participants are properly notified and consent rules are followed — some jurisdictions require every party to consent. Analysis should follow the same consent your recording tool captures. Check the rules where you and your buyers are located; this isn’t legal advice.

Will sales reps feel monitored by call analysis?

They will if the tool behaves like surveillance: leaderboards, manager-first access and personality scores. Adoption is better when reps see their own findings first, findings describe conduct rather than character, and every claim links to a moment they can replay and disagree with.

Can AI update the CRM automatically from sales calls?

Yes. AI can extract structured facts from a call — next steps, stakeholders, timeline, objections, budget signals — and write them to CRM fields, with each value traceable to the moment it came from. The value is accuracy the record never had when reps typed it from memory.
