Deal risk lives in behavior, not in fields

By the time a stalled deal shows up in the pipeline report, it has usually been stalling for weeks. The close date slips last. What slips first is behavior: response times stretch, the champion's replies get shorter, the decision maker stops attending, the same objection surfaces on a third call without ever being resolved.

None of that is in the CRM, because none of it is a field. It's a sequence — and sequences are what Bylda is built to read.

The stages say commit. The conversations say risk. Only one of them is right.

The signals Bylda watches

  • Stakeholder behavior — who is in the room, who joined late, who disappeared, and whether the economic buyer has actually engaged.
  • Objection trajectories — not just that a concern was raised, but whether the response resolved it or papered over it.
  • Engagement shifts — reply speed, meeting acceptance, forwarding behavior, and when they change.
  • Seller behavior — the follow-up that led with pricing when the concern was implementation; the discount offered before it was asked for.
  • Timing — commitments with dates, dates that move, and urgency that rises or quietly drains away.

Each signal is grounded in something that was actually said or done. Nothing is inferred from a stage's age or a rep's optimism — that's the difference between deal intelligence and a record-based system guessing from its own fields.

FAQ

Deal intelligence, answered.

Software that understands the state of a deal from actual commercial activity — conversations, email, meetings, stakeholder engagement — rather than from the fields a rep filled in. Good deal intelligence answers three questions: what changed, why it matters, and what to do next.

Stalls announce themselves in behavior before they show up in the pipeline: response times stretch, a champion goes quiet, the decision maker stops attending, an objection gets raised repeatedly without being resolved, or procurement takes over the conversation. Bylda watches for these patterns across every deal and flags the change with its evidence — usually weeks before the close date slips.

A specific, evidence-backed recommendation for what to do on a deal right now — for example, bringing the economic buyer into the conversation before revising pricing. Bylda derives it from the deal's observed behavior, and pairs it with the execution: the drafted follow-up, the created task, the updated CRM.

A score is a number without a reason. Deal intelligence is the reason: which behavior changed, what it means for this deal, and what the sensible response is. Bylda shows scores where they help, but every one traces back to specific observed moments a rep can verify.
