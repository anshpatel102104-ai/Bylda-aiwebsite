The 5-Question Lead Qualification Framework That Filters Buyers from Tire-Kickers

Not every inquiry deserves a full sales conversation. Spending 45 minutes with someone who was "just curious" is time that could have gone to a buyer ready to move this week. The difference between a growing business and an exhausted one is usually qualification — and the right five questions, asked the right way, make all the difference.

Why BANT Alone Doesn't Cut It Anymore

BANT — Budget, Authority, Need, Timeline — has been the standard qualification framework since the 1950s. It was designed for enterprise software sales with long sales cycles and multiple stakeholders. For service businesses in 2025, it falls short in two critical ways:

  • It front-loads the budget question, which feels interrogative and kills trust early in a conversation
  • It misses intent — the psychological readiness to buy, which is often more predictive than budget or timeline

The modern qualification framework for service businesses leads with intent and relationship, uses budget as a filter rather than an opener, and adds a geographic fit check that BANT ignores entirely. Here's how it works.

The 5 Questions (In Order)

1
Intent — What Are They Actually Trying to Solve?

The goal here is not just to confirm service interest but to understand the urgency and specificity of the problem. A vague inquiry ("I'm interested in your services") is very different from "I have a roof leak above my kitchen and it rained last night."

"What's going on that made you reach out today?"

Qualified signals: specific problem, emotional urgency, already knows what they need. Unqualified signals: vague curiosity, "just looking around," comparing options for a project that might not happen.

2
Timeline — When Do They Need It Done?

Timeline reveals how serious someone is. "Ideally this week" is a buyer. "Maybe sometime this year if the price is right" is a prospect for your long-term nurture sequence — valuable, but not worth a full sales slot today.

"How soon are you looking to get this taken care of?"

Route "this week or next" to booking. Route "next month or later" to a nurture sequence with a re-engagement trigger at the appropriate time.

3
Budget — Do the Numbers Work?

Ask budget third, not first. By the time you get here, you've already built a little rapport and context. Frame it as a range rather than a hard number — it reduces the defensive reaction.

"Have you had a chance to research pricing for this? Just so I can make sure what we offer lines up with what you're looking for."

If they're miles off on budget, it's better to know now than after a 45-minute consultation. Route severely misaligned leads to a resource or lower-tier option — don't just drop them.

4
Authority — Are They the Decision-Maker?

This matters most for higher-ticket services. If you're booking a $3,000 project and the person inquiring needs to "check with their spouse," you want to know before you go deep. It's not a disqualifier — it just changes how you schedule the next step.

"Is this something you can move forward on yourself, or would anyone else be part of that decision?"
5
Geographic Fit — Are They in Your Service Area?

Often forgotten in digital-first businesses, geographic fit is a hard filter for any service business with a physical footprint. A one-hour drive to a small job is a net loss — confirm coverage early.

"What area are you located in? I want to make sure we cover your neighborhood."

How AI Runs This Naturally

The power of AI qualification is that it asks these questions conversationally — not like a form, not like an interrogation. A text-based AI conversation covers all five questions in under 5 exchanges, feels personal, and routes the lead automatically based on responses.

The routing logic is simple:

  • Passes all 5 filters → Send booking link immediately, tag as "Hot Lead"
  • Passes intent + timeline, fails budget → Send educational content, re-engage in 30 days
  • Long timeline → Enter 90-day nurture sequence with monthly touchpoints
  • Out of service area → Politely close and optionally refer elsewhere
  • Decision-maker not present → Schedule a call that includes all parties

When this runs automatically on every incoming lead, you stop spending time on unqualified conversations and your close rate on actual sales calls climbs — because every person in your calendar has already been pre-screened.
