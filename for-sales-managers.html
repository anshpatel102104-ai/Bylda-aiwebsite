For Sales Managers & Heads of Sales

Coach the team without listening to every call.

You can review five calls a week. Your team has three hundred. Bylda audits all of them and hands you the short list: who needs help, what is repeating, where revenue is leaking, and what your best reps do differently.

The arithmetic nobody says out loud

Eight reps, six conversations a day each, five days a week. That is roughly three hundred conversations, and a manager with a forecast to run, escalations to handle, and their own pipeline reviews to sit in has time for maybe five of them.

So the sample you coach from is not representative — it is whichever calls happened to be on the calendar when you had a free hour. Whatever is systematically going wrong is, by definition, happening mostly in the calls you never hear.

You are not under-coaching because you lack discipline. You are under-coaching because 300 does not fit into 5.

What changes when every call is audited

Not more dashboards. A shorter list. Bylda reads every conversation and surfaces only what is both recurring and consequential — then tells you which two reps to start with.

Your Monday

Five questions, answered before you ask.

  • Who needs help? Ranked by behavior, not by attainment
  • Why? The specific conduct, with timestamps
  • What is repeating? Echoes across multiple reps
  • Where is revenue leaking? Located in the conversation
  • What do my best reps do differently? Made explicit, so it can be taught

Then one coaching priority for the week. Not forty metrics with no ranking — the thing most worth an hour of your attention, and the evidence for why.

Pipeline reviews stop being interrogations

The standard review runs on reconstruction: a rep describes calls from memory, a manager probes for the part being left out, and an hour disappears before anyone makes a decision. When every conversation has already been audited, the room starts at the decision instead.

The thing managers worry about, addressed

Auditing every call can become surveillance, and that would make your team worse, not better. We have made deliberate product choices against it: the rep sees their own audit before you do, findings describe conduct rather than character, strengths are reported alongside leaks, and there is no leaderboard anywhere in the product.

The test we hold ourselves to is whether a rep would open Bylda voluntarily. A tool your team resents produces gamed behavior and worse data — which defeats the entire point.
