How to Set Up GoHighLevel for a Service Business: The Complete Walkthrough

GoHighLevel is the most powerful CRM for service businesses — but it's also one of the most overwhelming platforms to set up from scratch. This walkthrough covers everything you need to go from a fresh GHL account to a fully operational system that captures, qualifies, and nurtures leads automatically. We do this for clients every week; here's the exact process.

Step 1: Account Setup and Business Configuration

Before anything else, configure your sub-account (your client account in GHL) with accurate business information. This matters because GHL uses these details in automated messages and sender configurations.

Go to Settings > Business Profile:

  • Business name, address, phone, and website
  • Business hours (used for automated responses and calendar availability)
  • Timezone — critical for automation triggers to fire at the right times
  • Logo and brand colors for email templates

Next, go to Settings > My Staff and add your team members with appropriate roles. Assign leads to specific team members for round-robin distribution if applicable. Every staff member should have their calendar connected at this stage.

Step 2: Pipeline Structure for Service Businesses

The pipeline is the core of your CRM. GHL's default pipelines don't match how service businesses actually operate. Here's the pipeline structure we use for most service businesses:

1
New Lead — Just entered system, not yet contacted
2
Contacted — AI or human has reached out, awaiting response
3
Qualified — Confirmed fit, ready for appointment
4
Appointment Booked — On the calendar
5
Proposal Sent — Quote or proposal delivered
6
Won / Active Client — Contract signed, work in progress
7
Nurture — Not ready now, in long-term sequence

Create this pipeline at CRM > Pipelines > Add Pipeline. Name it something clear like "Main Sales Pipeline."

Step 3: Calendar Setup

GHL's calendar is powerful but requires careful configuration. Go to Calendars > Calendar Settings and create your first calendar:

  • Calendar Type: "Round Robin" for multiple team members, "Personal" for a single person's availability.
  • Meeting Duration: Set your standard appointment length (15, 30, or 60 minutes).
  • Buffer Time: Add 10–15 minutes between appointments to avoid back-to-back booking.
  • Availability: Set your actual business hours and block off any recurring blocked times.
  • Booking Confirmation: Enable automatic email and SMS confirmation — these go out immediately when someone books.
  • Reminder Settings: Configure 24-hour and 1-hour reminders. This alone reduces no-shows by 40–60%.

Copy your calendar booking link from the calendar settings — you'll use this in automations and email templates throughout the system.

Step 4: Form Integrations

Every lead entry point needs to flow directly into GHL. Go to Sites > Forms to build your intake forms, or use GHL's webhook integration to capture leads from external sources:

  • Website forms: Build your contact/quote request form in GHL's form builder and embed the code on your website. Set the "on submission" action to create a contact and add them to your pipeline in Stage 1.
  • Facebook Lead Ads: Connect via the Facebook integration in Settings > Integrations. New FB leads auto-create contacts in GHL.
  • Google Ads leads: Use the webhook URL from GHL to receive leads from Google Lead Form extensions.
  • Zapier/Make: For any source GHL doesn't natively connect to, a simple Zap or Make scenario can push the lead data in via webhook.

Critically: every form submission should automatically trigger your "New Lead" automation workflow (built in the next step) — no manual action required.

Step 5: Automation Triggers

Go to Automation > Workflows > New Workflow. Your first and most critical workflow is the New Lead instant follow-up:

Trigger: Contact Tag Added → "new-lead" (add this tag in your form submission settings)

Action 1: Send SMS — "Hi [Contact.FirstName], thanks for reaching out to [Business Name]! [Personalized message]. Are you available for a quick call this week or next? Here's my calendar: [calendar link]"

Action 2 (Wait 5 min): Send Email — More detailed version with your value proposition and a direct booking link.

Action 3 (Wait 4 hours): If no reply → Second SMS follow-up with different angle.

Action 4 (Wait 24 hours): If still no reply → Third touch (email or voicemail drop depending on your preference).

Build separate workflows for: appointment confirmed, appointment reminder, no-show recovery, post-service follow-up, and review request.

Step 6: SMS and Email Sender Setup

Before any messages can go out, you need to configure your sending infrastructure:

SMS Setup: Go to Settings > Phone Numbers and purchase a local number in your area code. For businesses sending significant volume (500+ SMS/month), set up a toll-free number and complete toll-free verification to improve deliverability. Complete A2P 10DLC registration if you're using a local number — this is required by carriers and takes 5–7 business days.

Email Setup: Go to Settings > Email Services and connect a dedicated sending domain. Never send automation emails from a Gmail or generic address — use a subdomain like mail.yourdomain.com and complete the DKIM/SPF/DMARC DNS records. GHL walks you through this step-by-step. Proper email authentication is the difference between 95% inbox delivery and 30% spam folder delivery.

Test every automation by creating a test contact and walking through the full sequence before going live. Check message delivery, timing, personalization variables, and links on both desktop and mobile.
