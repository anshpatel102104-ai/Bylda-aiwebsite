SMS Marketing Best Practices for Service Businesses in 2025

SMS has a 98% open rate and most messages are read within 3 minutes of delivery. No other marketing channel gets anywhere close. But done wrong, SMS destroys trust, triggers spam complaints, and gets your number flagged by carriers. Here's how to do it right.

Compliance First: 10DLC and Opt-In Requirements

In 2025, SMS compliance isn't optional — it's enforced. Carriers actively filter non-compliant messages before they reach recipients. If your number isn't properly registered or your opt-in isn't documented, your messages may silently fail to deliver.

10DLC (10-Digit Long Code) is the current standard for business SMS in the United States. Any business sending automated text messages through a 10-digit local number must register their brand and campaign with The Campaign Registry (TCR). This registration tells carriers who you are, what you're sending, and why — which is why registered messages have far better deliverability than unregistered ones.

Registration is handled through your SMS provider (Twilio, GoHighLevel, etc.) and typically costs $4–$10/month plus a one-time setup fee. Failure to register doesn't just risk message filtering — it can result in your number being permanently blocked.

Opt-in requirements under TCPA require explicit written consent before sending automated marketing messages. "Explicit" means the person specifically agreed to receive texts from your business — pre-checked boxes, bundled consents, and implied consent don't qualify. Your opt-in language should state: who will text them, what type of messages, approximate frequency, that message and data rates may apply, and how to opt out (STOP).

Timing and Frequency

The legal window for SMS marketing under TCPA is 8am–9pm in the recipient's local time zone. But legal and optimal are different things. The highest-engagement windows for service business SMS are:

  • 10am–12pmPeak window. People are at work, checking phones regularly, not yet in afternoon mental fatigue.
  • 6pm–8pmStrong second window. People are done with work and actively on their phones during evening transition.
  • Avoid 8am–9amMorning commute is valid but conversion is lower — people are in transit mode, not decision mode.
  • Avoid weekendsOpen rates are fine but reply rates drop — people are less responsive to service inquiries on weekends unless it's an emergency service business.

Frequency for marketing SMS: 2–4 messages per month maximum for existing clients. For lead follow-up sequences, up to daily during the first 5–7 days is acceptable — these are high-intent contacts who just expressed interest. After that, dial back dramatically.

Message Length and Personalization

The standard SMS is 160 characters. Going over that splits into multiple message segments, which costs more and can display awkwardly on some devices. For transactional and conversational messages, aim for under 160 characters. For marketing messages, under 320 is acceptable if the content justifies it.

Personalization is not optional — it's the difference between a 4% and a 14% reply rate. At minimum, include:

  • First name (always)
  • Reference to their specific service or prior interaction where possible
  • Sender identification — who is texting them

What kills personalization: sending from a number labeled "5-digit short code" that obviously isn't a real person, writing in formal corporate language, and not acknowledging previous interaction history.

Calls to Action That Work

Every SMS should have one clear call to action. Not two, not three — one. The most effective CTAs for service businesses:

Reply-based "Reply YES to confirm" or "Reply with your zip code" — lowest friction, highest response rate
Link-based Direct booking link or review link — use shortened URLs, never raw long URLs
Question-based "Are you free Thursday or Friday?" — binary choice removes decision paralysis

Metrics That Matter

Most SMS platforms show open rates, but for service businesses the metrics that actually predict revenue are different:

  • Reply rate (target)8–15%
  • Link click-through rate (target)15–25%
  • Opt-out rate (healthy maximum)Under 2%
  • Conversion to booking (from reply)40–60%

If your opt-out rate exceeds 2%, your messaging is too aggressive, too frequent, or not relevant enough. If reply rate is below 5%, your personalization, timing, or CTA needs work. Treat these as diagnostic signals, not just reporting numbers.
